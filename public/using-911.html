Using 9-1-1

Public guidance about using 9-1-1.

Selected content adapted from the existing district website. Draft for review.

When to call 9-1-1

An emergency is a situation that requires immediate assistance from police, firefighters, or emergency medical services.

Examples from the district’s current guidance include a crime in progress, a fire, breathing problems, choking, unconsciousness, drowning, or a child locked in a vehicle.

What to expect when you call

  1. Dial 9-1-1.
  2. Clearly state where you are.
  3. Tell the operator what the emergency is and whether you need police, fire, or emergency medical assistance.
  4. Give your address and the nearest intersecting cross street. If an address is not available, describe landmarks such as mile markers, buildings, or street signs.
  5. Stay on the line until the operator tells you to hang up.

The operator may ask questions about the emergency and provide instructions. Follow those instructions and be available if the call-taker needs to call you back.

Text-to-911 is available in Shelby County.

Text-to-911 lets you send a text message to 9-1-1 emergency services instead of making a voice call. It can help when speaking aloud is dangerous or difficult.

Call 9-1-1 if you can. Text if you cannot safely make a voice call.

Texting can be especially helpful for:

  • People who are deaf, hard of hearing, or have a speech disability. Texting offers another way to request emergency help when a voice call is not possible.
  • People experiencing domestic violence. When speaking could alert an abuser, texting may provide a way to contact emergency services if it is safe to use your phone.
  • People unable to speak because of injury or illness. Texting may offer a way to request help during a medical emergency.

Non-emergency contacts

For situations that do not require an immediate emergency response, contact the public safety agency serving your location. For emergencies, call 9-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a situation warrants calling 9-1-1?
Any situation that is life-threatening or requires immediate assistance from police, fire, or emergency medical services warrants a 9-1-1 call. A house fire is one example. Read more about when to call 9-1-1.
If I’m in doubt about whether to call, should I still call?
Yes. It is better to call and let the 9-1-1 call-taker determine whether you need assistance than to risk someone’s life by not taking action.
What if I call 9-1-1 by mistake?
Stay on the line and tell the call-taker what happened so they know there is no emergency. Hanging up without speaking may lead the call-taker to believe you are in danger and send police to your location.
What are the costs of unnecessary 9-1-1 calls?
Prank calls and obvious non-emergency calls waste time and resources. They can occupy call-takers and telephone lines needed by people experiencing real emergencies.
Who pays for 9-1-1?
Tennessee funds 9-1-1 in part through a surcharge on covered communications services.

How can people who are deaf, hard of hearing, or have a speech disability contact 9-1-1?

Text-to-911 is available in Shelby County.

If you use a wireless phone or another mobile device in an emergency:

  • Always contact 9-1-1 by making a voice call, if you can.
  • If you are deaf, hard of hearing, or have a speech disability, and you are somewhere Text-to-911 is not available, use a TTY or a telecommunications relay service, if possible.
How can someone who does not speak English use 9-1-1?
The call-taker can add an interpreter to the call. The caller may hear the call-taker briefly speaking with the interpreter and hear clicking sounds before the interpreter addresses them. Remain calm and stay on the line while the interpreter joins.
Internet phone service: what should I know about calling 9-1-1?

VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol) carries telephone calls over an internet connection. Keep the address registered with your phone provider complete and current, including an apartment or suite number.

How does 9-1-1 know my location?

Some VoIP services use a registered physical address. Systems with dynamic emergency location capabilities can update location information as a device moves. Ask your provider or phone-system administrator how your service supplies your location, especially when you move a phone or work remotely.

A dispatchable location includes a street address and additional details, such as a floor, suite, or room, needed to locate the caller. Do not assume every internet phone automatically supplies this information.

Kari’s Law and RAY BAUM’S Act

Kari’s Law requires covered multi-line telephone systems to support direct dialing of 9-1-1 without an outside-line prefix such as “9.” The FCC rules apply to systems manufactured, imported, offered for first sale or lease, first sold or leased, or installed after February 16, 2020.

Covered systems must also be configured to notify a central location at the facility or another designated person or organization, on-site or off-site, when this can be done without a hardware or software improvement.

FCC rules implementing RAY BAUM’S Act address delivery of dispatchable location with 9-1-1 calls. Requirements vary by service and device type. Businesses should work with their provider or system administrator to verify direct dialing, notifications, and emergency location configuration.

Using 933 to verify your emergency address

If your VoIP provider supports 933, dial it from the phone you want to check and listen to the automated callback-number and emergency-address information.

If the address or phone number is incorrect or missing, contact your IT department or VoIP vendor to have it corrected. They can also confirm the full registered address, including floor, suite, or room details that an automated readback may omit.

933 availability and features vary by provider. A successful 933 call does not prove that a live 9-1-1 call will reach the correct center. If 933 is unavailable, ask your IT department or vendor how to check your emergency address.

How to test VoIP 9-1-1 safely

  1. Arrange the test first. Contact your local 9-1-1 center using its non-emergency number and ask how and when to place a test call. Do not dial 9-1-1 just to schedule a test.
  2. Follow the center’s instructions. At the approved time, identify the call immediately: “This is a scheduled VoIP 9-1-1 test. There is no emergency. Can you verify my callback number and location?”
  3. Confirm the details. Ask whether the call reached the correct center and verify the callback number, street address, city, and relevant floor, suite, or room. Follow the call-taker’s instructions before ending the call.
  4. Document the result. Record the test time, phone or extension tested, information confirmed, and any problems. Retain the provider’s call detail record, if available, with your test notes. A call record alone does not prove that the correct location was delivered.

If the call reaches the wrong center or displays an incorrect address or number, contact your provider or system administrator immediately. Arrange any follow-up test with the 9-1-1 center.

Can an unregistered or misconfigured VoIP phone still call 9-1-1?

It may, but do not rely on it. Depending on the provider and configuration, a call may reach a national emergency call center that must obtain your location and transfer you, reach the wrong local center, or fail to connect. Missing or incorrect location information can delay help. Keep your emergency address current and resolve configuration problems with your provider.
